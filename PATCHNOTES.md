## 13 November 2026: Standings

- **The standings, at the top right of the game:** every player's cities, kills and deaths, by colour, the most
  cities first. Kills and deaths count units; hover a number for the gold it was worth. Disbanding a unit is no
  death, unless an enemy hit it just before. Click **Standings** to fold it away, and again to bring it back: your
  browser remembers it.

## 12 November 2026: Chat

- **The waiting room has a chat**, beside the game's card: everyone in the room, players and spectators, by
  nickname. Whoever comes in reads the last messages.
- **Chat in the game:** press **Enter**, type, Enter again to send (Esc closes it). New messages show above the
  minimap for a few seconds, each player in their colour ("Red", "Teal", ...; yours as "You"), never by nickname:
  nobody learns who is who. Enter also opens every message so far, to scroll back.
- **Spectators** talk among themselves while the game runs: players don't see what they write. Once the game is
  over everyone is named, in the chat too.

## 12 November 2026: Roads

- **A new technology, Roads** (150 gold, 60 s), on a new **Land** tab of the Tech window: your land units march
  **20% faster** in every region you hold whole, that is, every city of it. Units already on the march speed up
  at once. Lose one city of a region and its roads no longer work for you. Ships are not affected.
- The Region line of a city's panel and F1 → Economy mention it. Click another player's unit and its panel shows
  its speed where it stands, with "on roads" while it has the bonus: their Roads work like yours.

## 12 November 2026: A new help (F1)

- **A wiki of the game:** F1 (or Help) now opens a help with its pages listed on the left, in three parts:
  Getting started (Welcome, Your screen), Controls (Selecting, Moving, Fighting, Camera & alerts, All keys) and
  The game (Cities, Units, Navy, Economy, Technologies, Honor & traditions, Winning, Liberator). A search box finds
  the pages that mention what you type. It opens on the page you read last.
- **See it done:** almost every control and rule has a little animation, played on the real map like a recording
  of a game: the pointer clicking, dragging a frame, right-clicking; the keys lighting up as they are pressed; and
  your units obeying, in your colour. Selecting, moving in formation (and shaping it with a drag), queueing
  orders, attacking, attack-moving, holding, stopping, disbanding, taking a city, training, rally points,
  upgrades, going on board, landing, Autoload, battleships, harbour repairs, a whole region, Heroes and Healers. A
  line under each says what is happening; ↻ plays it again.
- **Always up to date:** every number on its pages is the game's current one, unit and upgrade tables included,
  with the game's own icons. Details for those who want them are in tooltips: hover a dotted word or a small i.
- **Your screen** shows the top bar with its parts numbered and explained, and what each panel is for.

## 12 November 2026: A guide for your first steps

- **Know your colour:** the game now tells you which colour you are as it starts ("You are Red").
- **First steps:** a box at the top walks you through your first conquest, one step at a time: click one of
  your cities and press **Q** to train infantry, then send them to storm a grey city nearby. Taking it ends the
  guide. Do anything else instead and it gets out of your way; × closes it too.
- **While it trains:** the guide says where your infantry is being trained and how many seconds it has left.
- **Where to click:** in your first game since opening the tab, a gold arrow points at one of your cities and the
  Infantry button glows once you have selected it. Once your first unit is out, your units are ringed in gold until
  you select them, and a red arrow points at the grey city to storm.
- **Training progress on the buttons:** a unit's button in the Units box now shows a gold bar filling up while
  that unit trains in the selected city.
- **H** no longer appears in the welcome message: it holds position and has not jumped between cities for a
  while.

## 12 November 2026: New game defaults

- **New games start with different options** (the host can still change them all in the waiting room):
  - **Cities to win:** 140, up from 120.
  - **Countdown:** *From turn 10*: the target stays put for the first 10 turns, then drops by one city at the end
    of every turn. It used to count down from the start.
  - **Start gold:** 60, up from 50.

## 12 November 2026: Ballistas and cannons

- **Battleships shoot ballistas:** their shots are ballista bolts now, and a bolt does its damage when it lands,
  not when it is fired. You see it fly.
- **No dodging:** a bolt follows its target, a ship or troops on land, and always hits, unless the target dies
  first.
- **Warships:** the technology that unlocks battleships is now called Warships (same price and time).
- **Gunnery, a new Sea technology** (200 gold, 60 s, after Warships): your battleships get cannons, the ones
  already at sea too. At troops ashore and at cities they fire shells instead of bolts (at ships, still bolts). A
  shell follows its target like a bolt and bursts there: the target takes the whole damage, and every other enemy
  within 14 km of it takes 20% of it. A shell at a city bursts on its walls and does no
  harm to the units around it. You can tell a battleship with cannons by the crossed cannons on its sail and the
  row of gun ports along its side.
- **Shots outlive their ship:** a shot in the air still lands, and counts for you, if your battleship sinks first.
- **Repairs you can see:** a ship repairing in harbour, at the faster rate, shows its crew at work: a hammer
  knocking at its stern and a saw going at its bow.
- **Bigger harbours:** ships now repair at the faster harbour rate within 60 km of the pier of one of your coastal
  cities, up from 40 km.

## 12 November 2026: Formation width

- **Drag to set the width:** when you hold the right button and drag, the length of the drag now sets how wide the
  formation is: twice as wide as the drag is long. A short drag lines your army up in a narrow, deep column; a long
  one in a wide line of a few rows. The grey ghosts show it as you drag. Cavalry wings and the middle share the
  width by their numbers, so they stand about as deep. Ships too. A plain click still lays the formation out about
  twice as wide as deep.

## 12 November 2026: Cliffs you can see

- **Where you can't land now shows:** coast where transports can't take units on board or land them was drawn as
  a plain dark line, hard to tell from the rest. It is now drawn as cliffs: a ragged rock face dropping into the
  sea, with surf at its foot and a shadow on the water, nothing like the teal shallows and sand of a beach. Tiny
  islets with no room to land on look the same. On the minimap it stays a plain line.

## 12 November 2026: Spectators

- **Watch any game:** every game in the lobby has a **Watch** button, waiting or already in progress. You see the
  map as the players do, every empire by its colour, but play no part: no gold, cities or units of your own, and no
  orders. **Leave** takes you back to the lobby.
- **Still 20 players:** spectators take no player's place. Up to 10 can watch one game at once.
- **In the waiting room** spectators are listed under the players. **Play** takes a free place in the game, and a
  player can press **Watch instead**.
- **Who is watching:** the lobby lists each game's spectators by nickname ("Watching: Sue"). Inside the game nobody
  is told, and spectators know players only by colour, like everyone else, until the results reveal them.
- Spectators follow the game when its host leaves, keep watching after a page refresh, and see the results at the end.

## 12 November 2026: Under attack

- **Know when you're attacked:** when your units or one of your cities come under attack where you aren't looking,
  the spot pings orange on the minimap, the feed says where ("Your units are under attack near Lyon") and a battle trumpet
  sounds.
- **Without a flood of messages:** you hear about each battle once, not about every hit: it counts once it has
  cost a little, and is over after 25 seconds of quiet. Battles you can see on screen, and fights you sent your units
  into (attack, attack-move), don't alert. The feed speaks at most every 10 seconds and the trumpet every 30; battles
  starting in between share one line ("Your units are under attack in 3 places").
- **These always get through:** your city under attack and your transport under fire with units on board alert at
  once, at the first hit.
- **Shift + Space** now goes through your last 5 alerts, newest first: your cities captured, your cities under
  attack, your units under attack. Each press goes to the next older one ("2/5 · Red captured your city Lyon" in the
  feed), then back to the newest. A battle still going on is shown where the fighting is now.

## 12 November 2026: Gold in the results

- **How much gold everyone had:** the results screen has a new **Gold** graph: the gold each player had in their
  treasury at the end of every turn. Pick it above the graph in the Players tab; a player's own page shows it next

## 11 November 2026: Smoother big battles

- **No more stutter with a big army selected:** the panel of selected units was built again from scratch every time
  one of them died: with 470 units selected that took about 18 ms, a whole frame, and in a battle it happened up to
  10 times a second. Now only the fallen units' icons leave the panel (about 2 ms).

## 11 November 2026: Eternal Glory costs more

- **Eternal Glory:** a unit paid with honor now costs **3 times its gold cost**, not 2 (Infantry 36, Archers 30,
  Cavalry 60, Knight 450, Transport 30, Battleship 600).

## 11 November 2026: Formations

- **See where they go:** hold the right button with units selected and grey, see-through ghosts of your units show
  where each of them will stand. Let go to give the move; Esc while holding cancels it.
- **Turn the formation:** drag while holding the right button and the formation turns to face the way you drag; an
  arrow shows it. Without a drag it faces the way the group goes, as before. Works with A (attack-move) and Shift
  (queued moves) too.
- **Infantry in front, archers behind, cavalry on the wings:** a mixed group now lines up with the infantry in front,
  the archers in the rows behind it and Healers at the very back. Cavalry and knights split into two wings on the left
  and right. A Hero stands with its kind. At sea, battleships sail ahead of the transports.
- **Big armies keep their formation:** each unit keeps its place once there. Pushed off it by units walking through,
  or held up on the way by the crowd, it walks back to it. Before, a big army ended in a mixed crowd: units stopped
  wherever the crowd held them up and never got to their places.
- **One formation per order:** a whole army sent somewhere forms one formation, however spread out it is. Before,
  parts more than 150 km apart each formed their own round the same place, on top of each other.
- **Units at their places make way:** your units standing in formation step aside for your units walking through,
  and close up behind them.
- A move order is now given when you let go of the right button, not when you press it. Attacks, going on board and
  rally points still happen on the press.

## 11 November 2026: Gold percentage, start gold

- **Gold percentage per player:** in the waiting room the host sets a percentage for each player, next to their name
  (100% by default, 10–500%), to even out players of different strength: less gold for a strong player, more for a
  weaker one. The gold the player earns in the game is scaled by it: city pay every turn (regions and Farms
  included), liberator pay and smuggled gold, and kill gold. The start gold is the same for everyone, and refunds
  give back exactly what was paid.
- **Start gold** is a new game option in the waiting room: the gold every player starts with (50 by default).
- Everyone in the waiting room sees the percentages that are not 100%. In the game you see only your own: the feed
  tells you at the start, your income in the top bar includes it, and the gold tooltip and F1 → Economy mention it.
  Other players' percentages stay secret, so they can't tell who is who.

## 11 November 2026: Autoload

- **Navigation:** a new technology on the Sea tab of the Tech window (120 gold, 45 s). All your ships sail 20%
  faster, transports and battleships alike, the ones already at sea too. It needs Seafaring first, like Gunnery.
  A battleship still takes 1.5 s to gather speed after a stop, but its full speed is higher.
- **Smuggling takes it all:** a liberator now gets the whole income of each city they smuggle from, not half of it.
  Its owner gets nothing from that city while the mark lasts.
- **Rebellion technologies at half price for the fallen:** as a liberator you pay half for Supporters, Influencer and
  Smuggling (the Tech window shows the full price struck out). Levels ordered before you fell keep the price you
  paid, and cancelling always gives back exactly what was paid. Sea technologies cost the same for everyone.
- **Supporters costs 120 gold per level** (it was 100 / 180 / 260): 60 per level as a liberator.
- **Autoload (L):** a new button next to Unload in the panel of your transports. Press it and the selected transports
  take your idle land units within 200 km of them on board, with no need to select the troops. Select a whole fleet
  and it shares out the army around it: each unit goes to the nearest transport that still has room, and each
  transport sails to the free beach nearest to its own share, so the fleet spreads along the shore instead of
  queuing at one beach.
- Only idle units go: units walking somewhere, fighting, holding position or already on their way to a transport
  keep their orders. The feed says how many units head to how many transports.
- **L works for land units too (Board transports):** select an army and press L (or the Board transports button in
  its panel), and its units find your transports with room within 500 km and go on board, each to the nearest one,
  whatever they were doing. Only transports lying idle or already waiting at a beach answer; one sailing somewhere
  or landing units is left alone. With units and transports selected together, the units
  ask: a free transport nearby takes them, even when the selected ones are full.
- **Enemies no longer see how many units your transports carry:** the number on the boat and the On board count
  in its panel are for you only. Other players see a boat and nothing more.
- **Shift with loaded transports: park here, then go there.** Hold Shift and click on the water to give your
  transports a path; the first click on land, with or without Shift, is where they park and land the units. Every
  later Shift + right-click **on land** is for the units on board: once ashore they set off from the beach for the
  places you Shift-clicked, or attack the enemy units and cities you Shift-clicked (A + Shift: attack-move), one
  after another, without marching to the parking place first. With no such orders they march to it, as before.
  Shift + right-click **on the water** after that is the transports' own again: they sail there once everyone is
  ashore. Selected transports show their path, where they park, and the units' way on from the beach.
- **Big armies no longer gather before they march:** a group sent somewhere (move or attack-move, A + right click)
  used to follow one route, planned for the unit in its middle, so every unit first walked to that route's first
  corner, even with nothing in its own way, and a big army jammed there, some units for good. Now each unit goes
  its own way to its place in the formation: straight when it can, otherwise round what is in its way, sharing
  the way only with units close to it.
- **No more units pressed against city walls** at the end of a march: a formation spread over a city (a big army
  is 250 km wide) used to give some units places inside its walls, which they never reached. Those places are now
  just outside the walls.
- **A landing fleet no longer sends its army to one spot:** transports sent to land at one place use beaches all
  along the coast, and each gave its own units the same few places round the target, so hundreds of units headed
  for one spot and jammed there. Now everyone on board gets a place of their own in one formation for the whole
  fleet, as seen from where their transport lands them, and each finds its own way there from its beach. The
  same goes for what you Shift-click on land for them while they sail.
- **Formations by the coast stay on land:** places that fell in the sea or in the mountains used to send their
  units to the very centre of the formation. They now go to the free open ground nearest to them.
- **Storming a port lands everyone:** by ports such as Belfast, Dublin or Plymouth the beach lies next to the walls,
  and a full transport storming the city landed only 4 to 6 units: the rest stayed on board, to go down with it
  under the city's guns. Now everyone steps ashore at once, squeezing in among the others by the beach (the crowd
  pushes itself apart on land) or a little farther along the coast. Transports still arriving after the city has
  fallen land everyone too, instead of waiting for room on a beach full of victors.

## 10 November 2026: Regions in the city panel

- **Hover the Region stat** in the city panel and the whole region lights up on the map and the minimap: its land
  washed in light, its outline traced in gold. It fades in with a quick wave of light from the city out to the
  region's borders, which flash as it passes, and a ring of light spreads round each city of the region as the wave
  reaches it. The region's name shines in gold over everything on the map, cities and units included, with a gleam
  running across its letters. Move the mouse away and it all fades out.
- **Neutral cities show it too:** how many cities of the region you hold, and which are still to take. A spectator
  or liberator sees how many cities the region has and who holds each.
- The stat now updates as soon as another city of the region changes hands.

## 10 November 2026: Mountains

- **Mountains:** the great ranges of Europe, North Africa and Anatolia are on the map, and **no unit can cross them**:
  the Alps, Pyrenees, Cantabrian Mountains, Sistema Central, Sierra Nevada, Massif Central, Apennines, Dinaric Alps,
  Balkan Mountains, Rila, Rhodopes, Pindus, Carpathians, Scandinavian Mountains, Scottish Highlands, the ring round
  Bohemia, Caucasus, Urals, Taurus, Pontic Mountains, Atlas and Rif. Armies march round them or through their
  **passes**: Mont Cenis, Gotthard and Brenner across the Alps, Somport, Dukla, Shipka, Darial, the Cilician Gates
  and more. A move order into the mountains takes your units to the open land nearest to the spot.
- Mountains keep 12 km off the sea, so a road always runs along the coast past them, and 35 km off every city. They
  wall no city in: every city can still reach every city of its landmass by land, as before.
- Close up they are drawn as rows of hill signs on rocky ground, as on an old map; zoomed out, on the minimap and
  on the results timeline, as darker ground. They are solid: neither the colour of the region nor its borders show
  through them. What you see is exactly where units can't go.
- **Paint mountains / Erase mountains** in Edit map (admins): drag over the land to raise mountains or clear them,
  the built ones too. They block units at once in every game, and are saved for every game after. They keep 35 km
  off every city, and a stroke that would wall in a city or any big stretch of land is refused (a small valley it
  closes round is filled). They may reach the water, to close a road along the coast: the coast under them becomes
  cliffs, where no ship docks.
- **Nothing shoots over mountains:** archers, battleships and cities hit only what they can see past them. A battleship
  told to shell a target behind mountains sails to water with a clear line of fire to it (with none in range, the
  order is dropped); an archer walks round until it has a clear shot.
- **Norway's coast is simpler:** from Stavanger round the North Cape the fjords are filled and the coast runs across
  their mouths. Lofoten, Vesterålen and the islands by Tromsø stay islands, smoothed, and the tiniest skerries are
  gone. Bergen, Bodø and Tromsø keep their ports.

## 10 November 2026: Battleships

- **Gunnery:** a new technology on the Sea tab of the Tech window (150 gold, 60 s). It needs Seafaring first: you
  can order it as soon as Seafaring is researched or queued, and taking Seafaring out of the queue takes it out too.
- **Battleships (N):** warships built in coastal cities once you have Gunnery, for 200 gold. 500 HP, range 60, speed
  32. They shell ships, troops on the shore and cities, like archers do on land: one shot of **30 damage every 1.5
  seconds**. The guns reload while the ship sails, so you can shoot, move and shoot again: pull it back after a shot,
  and it fires the moment you order the attack again once it has reloaded. Attack-move (A + right click) works for
  them; a plain move never shoots. They are heavy: from a standstill, also after stopping to shoot, they need 0.75 s
  to reach full speed, so a ship that keeps running gains on one that stops to fire. In harbour they repair at twice
  their own regeneration (2 HP per second), not at the transports' 5.
- **Battleships take coastal cities:** a shot that brings a coastal city's HP to zero captures it, as any attack
  does. An inland city within their range goes down to 1 HP and no further: land troops to take it. A city
  doesn't regenerate between a battleship's shots (it still does under attacks that hit every second), so it can't
  win back between shots what it loses. Cities still outrange battleships (70 km against 60).
- Battleships fly a two-masted warship with a row of gun ports, and their sail carries crossed cannons. Every shot is
  drawn: smoke at the guns, the cannonball's arc, and a splash on the water or a burst of fire on land.
- **Attack-move goes for the nearest enemy:** units on attack-move no longer chase the first enemy they picked as if
  ordered to attack it. Once it is out of reach, they go for whichever enemy is nearest now, and when it gets out of
  sight they let it go and carry on to where they were sent. An attack order still follows its target anywhere.
- **A + right click on an enemy city** now attack-moves to it: your units fight the defenders they meet on the way,
  then the city. Before, it was a plain attack on the city, and the units walked past the enemy troops to reach it.
  A right click without A still orders the attack on the city alone.

## 10 November 2026: North Africa and Greenland

- **North Africa joins the war:** four new regions across the Mediterranean, 13 cities, most of them well inland,
  out of reach of ships' guns: **Morocco** (Tangier, Rabat, Marrakesh, Talsint), **Tlemcen** (Naâma, Relizane,
  Laghouat), **Algiers** (Constantine, Bou Saâda, Djamaa) and **Ifriqiya** (Sfax, Le Kef, Tozeur).
- **Cities farther from the sea in Spain:** Oviedo moves south over the mountains, Granada north, and Córdoba
  north-east to make room; all three are now about 100 km or more from the coast. Coimbra and Marseille are gone.
- Ships can no longer sail along the very bottom edge of the map, behind North Africa: the land grid there was a
  strip of sea.
- **Greenland:** brought down into the north-west corner of the map, with six cities: Tasiilaq and Ittoqqortoormiit
  on the coast, Sermersuaq, Summit, Eismitte and Schweizerland on the ice sheet. Iceland moved down to make room for
  it, and has a fourth city, Vík.
- **Islands of the North Atlantic:** Jan Mayen, off Greenland, is four times as big and has two cities of Norway,
  Olonkinbyen and Beerenberg. The Faroes are one island now, with Tórshavn, a region of its own.
- **Crete** is a third bigger, with Chania in the west and Agios Nikolaos in the east instead of Heraklion.
- **More room in central Europe and the Balkans:** 29 cities that stood too close to their neighbours are gone:
  Luxembourg, Eindhoven, Lille, Strasbourg, Freiburg, Bremen, Kassel, Leipzig, Erfurt, Plzeň, Bern, Geneva,
  Innsbruck, Ljubljana, Turin, Verona, Bologna, Nitra, Osijek, Dubrovnik, Kaunas, Plovdiv, Constanța, Edirne,
  Skopje, Larissa, Patras, Iași and Heraklion. **Saxony is gone:** Dresden now belongs to Prussia. **Macedonia is
  gone:** Tirana stands farther east, towards Skopje. Greece gets Kalamata in the Peloponnese. Venice and
  Switzerland are one-city regions.
- **France and Prussia thinned out:** Paris had Rouen and Orléans each just over 100 km away. Rouen is gone, and
  Orléans makes way for **Tours**, farther down the Loire: France is now Paris, Tours and Reims. Prussia loses
  Magdeburg, so close to Berlin and Hanover.
- **Wider straits:** the Bosporus and the Dardanelles are now 12 to 20 km of open water instead of a thin crack,
  easy to see and to sail. Istanbul stands a little farther west, on its shore.
- **No more Cyclades:** the small islands of the central Aegean, and those north-east of Euboea, are gone.
- **The Middle East and the steppe join the war:** no more grey land in the south-east. Seven new regions:
  **Georgia** (Sukhumi, Tbilisi), **Persia** (Yerevan, Ganja, Urmia), **Syria** (Aleppo, Damascus,
  Palmyra), **Mesopotamia** (Raqqa, Mosul, Tikrit, Haditha, Rutba), the **Levant** (Acre,
  Jerusalem, Azraq), **Arabia** (Turaif, Arar) and the **Nogai Horde** on the Kazakh steppe (Chapaev, Inder,
  Saraichik).
- **The north filled in:** Krasnoshchelye on the Kola Peninsula and Verkhnyaya Toyma on the Dvina join Pomorye,
  Nikolsk takes Veliky Ustyug's place in Muscovy, Brønnøysund joins Norway and Mora Sweden.
- **Land without a city is shared out:** Slovenia, North Macedonia, Luxembourg, Kosovo and the microstates are split
  between the cities nearest to them, like the rest of the map, instead of going whole to one city (or staying
  neutral).
- **More cities in the east:** Sivas, Van and Urfa in Anatolia; Veliky Ustyug in Muscovy, in the gap between
  Arkhangelsk, Ukhta and Nizhny Novgorod; Tuapse and Grozny in the Golden Horde, in the Caucasus; Pustozersk on the
  lower Pechora and Khanty-Mansiysk beyond the Urals in Yugra.
- **Inland Scandinavia:** Kiruna and Vilhelmina in Sweden, Otta in Norway.
- **Pomorye:** a new region on the White Sea, split off from Novgorod: Murmansk, Arkhangelsk and the new city of
  Kem. Novgorod keeps Saint Petersburg, Novgorod and Petrozavodsk.
- **White Ruthenia:** a new region split off from Lithuania: Minsk, Brest-Litovsk, Gomel and Vitebsk. Lithuania
  keeps Vilnius and Klaipėda.
- The map now has **248 cities in 71 regions**.
- **All of the coast is beach:** transports take units on board and land them anywhere on the coast, every island's
  too. The cliffs are gone (admins can still paint some in Edit map).
- **The view goes past the edges:** the camera can now be moved a little beyond every edge of the map, so the land
  along it, such as North Africa at the bottom, can be seen clear of the minimap and the panels. Where the map's
  edge cuts the land off, it is no longer drawn as a coast.
- Region names on the map are darker, with a faint white border round the letters: easier to read.
- **Edit map: region names.** Admins can drag a region's name to a new place, make it bigger or smaller with the
  mouse wheel, and double-click it to put it back where the game would place it. Everyone sees it at once, and it is
  saved for every game.

## 10 November 2026: Surrender, and Restart for duels

- **Surrender and watch:** pressing **Leave** during a game now asks what you want. **Surrender and watch** gives up
  your empire (your cities become neutral, your units are gone) but keeps you in the game as a spectator, so you see
  how it ends. **Leave game** gives up and goes back to the lobby, as before. Players who surrendered show as
  "Surrendered" in the Players list and on the results screen.
- **The game survives its host:** when the host leaves, closes or refreshes their tab, or loses their connection,
  another player's browser takes the game over and everyone plays on. At most the last few seconds are lost (the
  host's browser saves the game every 5 seconds). A host whose tab closed or whose connection dropped keeps their
  empire and comes back as an ordinary player, by refreshing or with Rejoin in the lobby.
- **The last one in wins:** once everyone else has surrendered or left, the last player wins at once. A game started
  alone is not won this way, so you can still play by yourself to try things out; surrendering ends it.
- **Restart a duel:** a game of two players has **Restart** on its results screen. When both of you press it, the same
  game starts again right away, without going back to the lobby: new colours, the usual countdown. The screen shows
  when your opponent wants a rematch, or that they have left.
- **Cancel with C:** with cities selected, **C** cancels the last unit or building in each selected city's queue and
  gives its gold (or honor) back. Press it again to cancel the one before. With units selected, C still makes a Hero.
- **Last Stand is gone:** the tier II tradition and its effects (30% more damage, 30% less when outnumbered, the red
  glow) are removed. Eternal Glory is now the only tier II tradition.
- **No browser shortcuts in a game:** keys with Ctrl or Alt and the function keys no longer reach the browser while
  you play, so Ctrl + F, Ctrl + G, F3, Ctrl + S, Ctrl + P, Ctrl + H, Ctrl + D, Alt + Left, F5, Ctrl + Plus/Minus,
  Ctrl + wheel and the like do nothing (or only their in-game action). F11 still switches to full screen. Chrome
  does not let a page block Ctrl + W, Ctrl + T, Ctrl + N or Ctrl + Tab.

## 9 November 2026: Kill rewards, honor, winning at the end of the turn

- **Winning at the end of the turn:** holding the target number of cities no longer ends the game at once. The game
  checks it when the turn ends: whoever still holds the target then wins. Reach it in the middle of a turn and you have
  to keep it until the turn is over; if your enemies take cities back and you are below the target when the turn ends,
  the game goes on. The target checked is the one shown during that turn (the countdown lowers it right after).
  Several players holding it: the most cities win; an exact tie plays on to the next turn. The last player standing
  still wins at once.
- **Who is close to winning:** when a player is 25 cities or fewer from the target, everyone sees it at the top
  centre of the screen: "Maroon needs 10 more cities to win". Once a player holds the target, the line turns red and
  shows the time left in the turn and how many cities they can still lose: "Maroon wins when the turn ends (0:23)
  unless they lose 6 cities". Time to strike back.

- **Kills pay gold:** destroying an enemy unit pays you **25% of its cost** at once: Infantry 3, Archers 2.5,
  Cavalry 5, Knight 37.5, Transport 2.5. The top bar shows whole gold; halves still add up.
- **New resource, honor:** every kill gives you **the unit's full cost in honor** (Infantry 12, Archers 10,
  Cavalry 20, Knight 150, Transport 10), so your honor grows exactly like your Kills in the Players list. It is shown
  with a silver star in the top bar, right after your gold.
- Both follow the kills in the Players list: the last hit gets the kill, and your cities' kills are yours. Units on
  board a sunk transport count for whoever sank it. A disbanded unit pays the last player who hit it in the 20 seconds
  before it died. Kills by neutral cities pay nobody.
- **F1 → Economy** lists kills as a source of gold.

### Queues for research and traditions

- **Research queue:** order research while one is running and it is **queued** (the button says **Queue level N**).
  It is paid at once and starts by itself once the one before it is done, so you can line up several technologies,
  or several levels of one. A line under the Tech window's tabs lists the queue in order; **✕** takes one out and
  gives its gold back (and takes out the higher levels of that technology queued after it, as they need it).
- **Tradition queue:** the same in the Honor window: **Queue** a tradition while another is learned. Its row shows
  its place (**Queued #1**) and **Remove**, which gives the honor back. A tier II tradition can be queued once every
  tier I one is learned or queued ahead of it; removing a tier I one also removes the tier II ones queued.

### New: the Honor window, traditions and Heroes

- Click the honor star in the top bar (or press **O**) to open the **Honor** window. There you spend honor on
  **traditions**: learned once and kept for the whole game, one at a time (alongside your research). Cancelling gives
  the honor back.
- One row per tradition: its coat of arms in your colour, its name and what it is for, and the price and **Learn** on
  the right. Hover a row for the full details. The Players list and the results show each tradition as a small
  silver emblem with one part in that player's colour.
- **Cult of Heroes** (1500 honor, 30 s): any of your land units can now be made a **Hero**. Select one and press
  **C**, or **Make Hero** in its panel (**100 honor**). It becomes a Hero where it stands, keeping its orders and its share of
  HP: 300 HP and 15 damage per second, with **the speed and range of what it was**. Infantry make an Infantry Hero,
  archers an Archer Hero that shoots from afar, cavalry and knights a Cavalry Hero that rides. It wears a crown and a
  gold ring on the map.
- **A Hero's aura:** all the time, every land unit of yours within **80 km** of a Hero, the Hero too, deals **10% more
  damage**. Several Heroes don't add up. Your selected Hero shows how far it reaches.
- A Hero counts in the Kills and Losses stats as the gold of the unit it was made from. Cities never train Heroes.
- **Order of Mercy** (1500 honor, 30 s): any of your land units can now be made a **Healer** (**80 honor**, Make
  Healer in its panel). A weak fighter (120 HP, 5 damage per second) with a white cross, it heals every land unit of
  yours within 60 km, itself too, by **3 HP per second**, even in combat. Several Healers don't add up. Healed units
  glow green; your selected Healer shows its range. Only plain units can become a Hero or a Healer.
- **Brotherhood of Arms** (1500 honor, 30 s): Barracks can be paid with **30 honor** instead of 30 gold. The build
  column gets a second Barracks button in the Honor window's colours (or press **Shift + G**). It is the same building:
  units train twice as fast. Paid with honor or gold, Barracks now **build twice as fast** (6 s instead of 12 s). In the queue it shows a small silver star; cancelling it, or losing the
  city while it waits, gives the honor back.
- **Honored Dead** (1500 honor, 30 s): every unit of yours that falls gives you **25% of its cost in honor**
  (Infantry 3, Knight 37.5), whether an enemy, a neutral city or a sinking ship took it. Disbanded units don't count,
  unless an enemy hit them in their last 20 seconds.
- **Two tier II traditions** (4000 honor, 2 minutes each), written in red and tagged **Tier II**: each opens once you
  have learned all four tier I traditions, and they don't need each other. Starting one is not announced, but the
  Players list shows which one and its countdown; every player is told with a red banner once it is learned.
  - **Eternal Glory:** every unit can be trained for honor, at **2 times its gold cost** (Infantry 24, Knight 300): a
    **Pay with Gold / ★ Honor** switch in the Units box, or **Shift + the unit's key**.
  - **Last Stand:** all your units deal **30% more damage** (+40% near a Hero). A unit of yours with at least
    **twice as many enemy units as yours** within 80 km of it takes **30% less damage**, and glows red while it does,
    for everyone to see. Its emblem: a gold cuirass over two crossed swords.
- **Players list (Tab):** a new **Traditions** column shows every player's traditions (they are public now), with
  the tier II ones in red, and which one someone is learning with its countdown.
- **New sounds:** a choir humming in a cathedral when you learn a tradition, and a quill signing its name when your
  research is complete.

  the map: who held how many cities on that turn (and the change since the turn before), what happened in it

### New: lost-city alerts and a resizable minimap

- **The minimap can be resized:** drag its top-right corner. It keeps the map's shape and grows up and to the

- **Russia:** the Golden Horde is split. Kazan and Samara, with the new cities **Kungur** and **Ufa**, form the

- **Karasjok**, an inland city, holds the north of Norway (Finnmark).

### Balance

- **Unit limit doubled:** each player may now have **1000 units** (it was 500). Units on board transports count too.
  At the limit, cities pause training units until some fall; upgrades still build.
- **Infantry no longer blocks archer damage.** Archers now hit infantry for their full damage (it used to be 10%
  less).

### Fixes

- **The host no longer gets a head start.** The host's game used to start the moment it loaded, while the other
  players were still connecting, often 10–15 seconds later. Now everyone sees "Waiting for every player to load the
  game" with how many have, then a **10-second countdown**, and the game starts for everyone at once. Until then
  nothing moves and orders are not accepted, but you can look around the map and find your cities. A player who
  never connects holds up the start for 30 seconds at most; they can still join the game later.
- **No more freezes when you send a big army far away.** Sending a few hundred units across the map could stop
  the whole game for up to a second, for every player, and sometimes several times in a row: working out the way
  around a sea (Iberia to Scandinavia goes round the Baltic) searched most of the continent. Paths now know about
  those detours, so a long search takes a few milliseconds, and units waiting for a path no longer queue up behind
  each other. In a test with five players and 2,000 units, the longest pause fell from 1.8 seconds to about a tenth
  of a second (five big orders given at the same moment), and a typical one is now a few milliseconds.
- **Every unit of a big army sets off at once.** Units that could not see the route of their group (a city or the
  coast in between) used to stand still for up to a second before following it. Now they all start walking with the
  order.
- **Big battles cost the host less.** Keeping thousands of units from overlapping took more than half of the host's
  game time in a big battle; it now takes about a fifth of what it did.

### Network: 13–15 times less traffic

- **The host sends each player only what changed.** Every snapshot (10 a second) used to carry the whole game:
  all 210 cities and every unit on the map, even those standing still. Now it carries only the changes since the
  last one: units that moved, cities that were hit or upgraded, the production that progressed. The player's
  browser rebuilds the whole snapshot from them, exactly as the host made it: same rate, same precision, every unit
  on the map as before. Scores are sent the same way.
- **Compressed on direct links**, about three times smaller again (links through the server were compressed already).
- With the whole map in view, a player now gets about 0.15 Mbit/s in an 8-player game with 100 units each (it was
  2.2 Mbit/s), and about 0.35 Mbit/s with 20 players (it was 5.2). The **host's upload**, the real limit, falls from
  15 to about 1 Mbit/s for 8 players, and from 100 to about 7 Mbit/s for 20 players: a usual home connection can now
  host a full game.
- **A slow link no longer lags behind.** A player whose connection can't keep up used to get snapshots seconds late,
  piled up in a queue. Now the host skips a snapshot for them instead (the next one has everything), and the deaths
  and War Cries of a skipped snapshot come with the next one, so none is missed.
- **Faster page loads.** The game's files are sent compressed (about a third of the size), and a reload no longer
  downloads files that haven't changed.

## 8 November 2026: Tech tree

### New: game options

The host now sets the rules in the waiting room (**Game options**). Everyone else sees them, and the lobby list
shows them for every game.

- **Cities to win:** 120 by default. Anything from 11 up to every city on the map.
- **Countdown:** the target can shrink as the game goes on.
  - *From start* (the default): one city fewer at the end of every turn (120, then 119, then 118...).
  - *From turn N*: the target stays the same until turn N (10 by default) is over, then counts down.
  - *No*: the target never changes.
  - It never drops below 1 city. If it drops to several players at once, the one with the most cities wins. If they
    hold exactly as many, the game goes on until one of them pulls ahead.
  - The top bar shows your cities next to the current target, with ▾ while it counts down.
- **Death:** what happens to a player who loses every city and every unit.
  - *Liberator* (the default): see below.
  - *Spectator*: they are out and can only watch, as before.

### New: liberators

With **Death: Liberator**, a player who loses everything stays in the game as a liberator.

- They lose all their gold, then get **20 gold** at the end of every turn.
- They can take any one city on the map, neutral or another player's: click it and press **Liberate**. The city
  changes hands like a capture: its upgrades are lost, and its previous owner gets back the gold for its queue.
  This puts the liberator back in the game.
- A player who holds **5 cities or fewer** is protected. Liberators can't take any of their cities.
- A liberator can take a city at most once every **2 turns**. Falling again always costs all your gold.
- Liberators are never eliminated. With this option, you win only by holding the target number of cities, or by being
  the last player left after everyone else has pressed Leave.
- **F1 → Liberator** explains all of this in the game.

### New: technologies

Press **Tech** (or **T**) to open the Technologies window. Research costs gold up front and takes game time. You can
research one technology at a time. Cancelling gives the gold back.

The first tab, **Rebellion**, appears only in games with Death: Liberator. You can research these at any time, even
before you fall, but they only work while you are a liberator.

| Technology | Levels | Cost | Time | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supporters | 3 | 100 / 180 / 260 gold | 2 min each | +20 / +40 / +60 liberator gold per turn |
| Influencer | 3 | 100 gold each | 2 min each | Mark 2 / 3 / 4 cities instead of taking just one |
| Smuggling | 3 | 30 gold each | 1 min each | Steal from 1 / 2 / 3 cities |

- **Influencer:** you mark (influence) one city per turn. When you mark the last one, they are all liberated at once.
  **Liberate now** takes the marked cities earlier. Everyone sees the marks as a turning dashed ring in the
  liberator's colour, and the feed says which city is being stirred up. Only one liberator can mark a city. The
  protection for small empires is checked city by city at the moment of liberation.
- **Smuggling:** at the end of every turn, half of each marked city's income goes from its owner to you. Neutral
  cities pay nothing. A smuggled city shows a gold coin. Only one liberator can smuggle from a city, but one liberator
  may influence a city while another smuggles from it. Click a smuggled city again to stop.
- A liberator who is back in the game loses all their marks, so other liberators can mark those cities.

### New: regions

- The map is split into regions (Hungary, Wallachia, ...). Their names are printed on the map. Dark lines are the
  borders between regions, and faint lines divide the cities of one region.
- Hold **every city of a region** and each of them pays **+2 gold** per turn. Lose one and the whole region loses the
  bonus. A one-city region counts too.
- The city panel shows how much of its region the owner holds. The feed tells you when you complete or lose a region,
  and the end-of-turn message shows how much your regions paid.
- When you take the last city of a region, the region lights up for a few seconds. A wave of gold light spreads from
  that city, the region's outline glows, and every city in it bursts with a gold ring and **+2/turn**. The region's
  name shines in gold, and it also lights up on the minimap.

### New: hold position

- **H** with units selected: they hold position. They never walk, not even to chase, and only fight what comes within
  reach. Other units can't push them aside. A move, attack or stop order ends it.
- A dashed gold ring marks your units on hold.
- **H** no longer jumps to your next city.

### New: order queue

- **Shift + right click** with units selected queues a move, attack-move (hold A) or attack after the orders they
  already have. They carry them out one after another. This also works on the minimap.
- Units with nothing to do start the order at once.
- A group keeps its formation through every queued move.
- A queued attack starts when the unit gets there. If the target is already gone, the unit skips it and goes on to the
  next order.
- A right click without Shift, **S** or **H** replaces the whole queue.
- A unit can have up to 20 orders waiting.
- Selected units show their queued route as dashed lines: green for a move, orange for an attack-move, red for an
  attack.

### New: sounds

- A clock ticks in the last 3 seconds of every turn.
- A sword clash sounds when someone captures one of your cities, wherever it is on the map.
- Sound starts after your first click or key press (a browser rule).

### New: wall grades

The wall now comes in two grades. One button (or **A**) orders both:

| Grade | Cost | Time | City HP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wall I | 6 gold | 4 s | +100 |
| Wall II | 14 gold | 6 s | +150 more |

- Each order queues the next grade the city doesn't have yet: press **A** twice to queue Wall I and then Wall II.
  Together they cost 20 gold, take 10 s and give +250 HP. When a little extra HP is enough, stop at the cheap Wall I.
- With several cities selected, each one gets its next grade: Wall I where it has none, Wall II where Wall I is built
  or queued.
- Wall II needs Wall I built or on its way. Cancelling Wall I also cancels a Wall II queued after it, and both are
  refunded.
- A city with Wall II shows a thicker ring wall with turrets.

### Changes

- **Rally points:** new units now attack-move to the rally point, fighting anything they meet on the way.
  **Alt + right click** clears the rally points of the selected cities.
- **Help (F1)** now has pages: **Controls**, **Economy** (every way to get gold, from the current numbers, plus the
  regions you hold or are close to completing) and **Liberator**.
- **Unit panel:** click a unit icon to select only that unit (or only that type). **Ctrl + click** removes it from the
  selection. **Shift + click** marks icons, and the marked units become the selection when you release Shift.
  The panel is more compact and its icons show just the unit's emblem (crossed swords, bow, shield...), as over its
  cone on the map, so a big army is easy to read. It never covers more than the bottom of the screen: past its
  height limit the icons scroll. With many units selected, the line of control tips under the icons is hidden.
- **Feed:** the end-of-turn message shows liberator and smuggling gold, and player names in dark colours (Navy, Black,
  Maroon, Purple, ...) get a light backing so they stay readable.
- When you capture the city you were looking at, it is selected so you can build there at once.
- **Map:** a new city, **Simferopol** (Zaporozhia), brings the map to 208 cities. Borders are redrawn. Only region
  borders and the lines between cities of one region are shown, and modern national borders appear only where they
  divide two cities.
